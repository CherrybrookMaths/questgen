      $y$-intercept: when $x=0$, $y=e^{1}=e$.

      $x$-intercept: $e^{1-x}\ne0$, so $(x+1)e^{1-x}=0$ gives $x+1=0$, that is $x=-1$.

      Using the product rule, $\dfrac{d}{dx}(uv)=u'v+uv'$, with
      $$\begin{aligned}u&=x+1 & v&=e^{1-x}\\ u'&=1 & v'&=-e^{1-x}\end{aligned}$$
      $$\begin{aligned}\dfrac{dy}{dx}&=e^{1-x}+(x+1)e^{1-x}\times(-1)\\ &=e^{1-x}(1-x-1)\\ &=-xe^{1-x}\end{aligned}$$
      Stationary points where $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=0$: $x=0$, $y=e$.

      Product rule again, with
      $$\begin{aligned}u&=-x & v&=e^{1-x}\\ u'&=-1 & v'&=-e^{1-x}\end{aligned}$$
      $$\begin{aligned}\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}&=-1\times e^{1-x}-x\times e^{1-x}\times(-1)\\ &=e^{1-x}(-1+x)\end{aligned}$$
      When $x=0$: $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=-1\times e^{1}<0$, concave down.

      $\therefore (0,e)$ is a local maximum turning point.

      Points of inflection are possible where $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}=0$: $x=1$, $y=2\times e^{0}=2$, that is $(1,2)$.

      The concavity changes, so $(1,2)$ is a point of inflection.
