        The daylight hours increase fastest where $L'(t)$ is a maximum, so solve $L''(t)=0$.

        $$\begin{aligned} L'(t) &= -\dfrac{4\pi}{365}\sin\left[\dfrac{2\pi}{365}(t+10)\right] \\ L''(t) &= -\dfrac{8\pi^2}{365^2}\cos\left[\dfrac{2\pi}{365}(t+10)\right] \end{aligned}$$

        $$\begin{aligned} \cos\left[\dfrac{2\pi}{365}(t+10)\right] &= 0 \\ \dfrac{2\pi}{365}(t+10) &= \dfrac{\pi}{2},\ \dfrac{3\pi}{2} \\ t+10 &= 91.25,\ 273.75 \\ t &= 81.25,\ 263.75 \end{aligned}$$

        $$\begin{aligned} L'(81.25) &\approx -0.03 \\ L'(263.75) &\approx 0.03 \end{aligned}$$
