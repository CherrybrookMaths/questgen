    source: extracted
    latex: |
      Since $y=x^3$ is an odd function,
      $$\int_{-2}^{2}x^3\,dx=0$$
      The area under $y=x^3$ from $x=0$ to $x=2$ is above the $x$-axis, so
      $$\int_0^2x^3\,dx>0$$
      Option A adds an absolute value to this positive integral, and option C doubles it, so both are greater than $0$.

      Reversing the limits in option D (and using $x^3$ odd):
      $$\begin{aligned}\int_0^2x^3\,dx+\int_0^{-2}x^3\,dx&=\int_0^2x^3\,dx-\int_{-2}^{0}x^3\,dx\\&=2\int_0^2x^3\,dx\end{aligned}$$
      which is also greater than $0$.

      Since $y^{\frac{1}{3}}$ is also an odd function,
      $$\int_{-8}^{8}y^{\frac{1}{3}}\,dy=0$$
      so option B is equivalent.
