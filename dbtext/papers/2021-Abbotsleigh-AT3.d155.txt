      latex: |
        Solutions of $-e^x(x+1)=x^2-1$ are the intersections of $y=-e^x(x+1)$ with the parabola $y=x^2-1$.

        Both pass through $(-1,0)$ and $(0,-1)$, since $x^2-1=0$ at $x=-1$ and $x^2-1=-1$ at $x=0$.

        So $x=-1$ and $x=0$ are solutions. We check there are no others by comparing the two sides on each interval.

        For $x>0$: $e^x\ge 1+x$, so $-e^x(x+1)\le-(x+1)^2$, and
        $$-(x+1)^2-(x^2-1)=-2x(x+1)<0$$
        so the exponential curve is below the parabola: no solution.

        For $x<-1$: put $t=-x>1$. The equation $e^{-t}(t-1)=(t-1)(t+1)$ needs $e^{-t}=t+1$, impossible since $e^{-t}<1<t+1$: no solution.

        For $-1<x<0$: divide by $x+1>0$ to get $-e^x=x-1$, that is $e^x+x-1=0$. The function $e^x+x-1$ is increasing and equals $0$ only at $x=0$, so there is no solution in this interval.

        Therefore there are exactly $2$ solutions, option B.

      latex: |
        Using the product rule, $\dfrac{d}{dx}(uv)=u'v+uv'$, with
        $$\begin{aligned} u&=x & v&=e^{-x}\\ u'&=1 & v'&=-e^{-x}\end{aligned}$$
        $$\dfrac{dA}{dx}=x(-e^{-x})+e^{-x}(1)=e^{-x}(1-x)$$
        For stationary points $\dfrac{dA}{dx}=0$. Since $e^{-x}>0$, this gives $1-x=0$, so $x=1$.

        Test the sign of $\dfrac{dA}{dx}$ either side of $x=1$:
        $$\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}x&0&1&2\\\hline \dfrac{dA}{dx}&1&0&-0.135\end{array}$$
        The gradient changes from positive to negative, so $x=1$ gives a maximum.

        At $x=1$, $y=e^{-1}$.
        $$\therefore\ \text{length }1,\ \text{width }e^{-1}$$
