        When $\theta$ is close to either bound, $L \to \infty$ in $L = \dfrac{3}{\cos\theta} + \dfrac{4}{\sin\theta}$, but then the whiteboard is too long to make it around the corner.

        \exfigure{277_q31_s4}

        The whiteboard turning the corner is limited by the shortest possible value of $L$, so the angle that minimises $L$ must be found.

        Write
        $$L = \dfrac{3}{\cos\theta} + \dfrac{4}{\sin\theta} = 3(\cos\theta)^{-1} + 4(\sin\theta)^{-1}$$
        Differentiating:
        $$\begin{aligned} \dfrac{dL}{d\theta} &= -3(\cos\theta)^{-2}(-\sin\theta) - 4(\sin\theta)^{-2}(\cos\theta) \\ &= \dfrac{3\sin\theta}{\cos^2\theta} - \dfrac{4\cos\theta}{\sin^2\theta} \\ &= \dfrac{3\sin^3\theta - 4\cos^3\theta}{\cos^2\theta\sin^2\theta} \end{aligned}$$
        For stationary points $\dfrac{dL}{d\theta} = 0$.
        $$\begin{aligned} 3\sin^3\theta &= 4\cos^3\theta \\ \tan^3\theta &= \dfrac{4}{3} \\ \theta &= \tan^{-1}\left[\left(\dfrac{4}{3}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\right] \\ &= 0.833\ldots \text{ rad} \\ &= 47.74^\circ \end{aligned}$$
        The sign of $\dfrac{dL}{d\theta}$ is decided by the numerator $3\sin^3\theta - 4\cos^3\theta$ (the denominator is positive), so test either side of $\theta = 47.74^\circ$.

        \extable{Ts4804_1}

        The gradient changes from negative to positive.

        Hence $\theta = 47.74^\circ$ is a minimum turning point. When $\theta = 47.74^\circ$:
        $$\begin{aligned} L &= \dfrac{3}{\cos 47.74^\circ} + \dfrac{4}{\sin 47.74^\circ} \\ &= 9.8657\ldots \\ &\approx 9.87 \end{aligned}$$
        $$\therefore\ \text{the maximum possible length of the whiteboard is } 9.87 \text{ metres}$$
