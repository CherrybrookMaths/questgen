      In the adjacency matrix, the entry in row $X$, column $Y$ is the number of edges joining $X$ and $Y$. The graph has no loops, so every diagonal entry is $0$, and the matrix is symmetric.

      Reading the number of edges from the graph:
      $$\begin{aligned} A\text{ to }B &: 1 & A\text{ to }C &: 1 \\ A\text{ to }D &: 1 & B\text{ to }C &: 2 \\ C\text{ to }D &: 1 & B\text{ to }D &: 0 \end{aligned}$$

      So the matrix has rows $A: 0,1,1,1$; $B: 1,0,2,0$; $C: 1,2,0,1$; $D: 1,0,1,0$.

      Option B has $C$ to $C$ equal to $2$, but there is no loop at $C$. Option C is not symmetric (row $A$ is all zeros but $B$ to $A$ is $1$). Option D has $1$s on the diagonal, but there are no loops.

      Therefore the matrix matches the first table.
