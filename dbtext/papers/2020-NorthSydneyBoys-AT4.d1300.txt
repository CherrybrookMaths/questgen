        Using the quotient rule, $\dfrac{d}{d\theta}\left(\dfrac{u}{v}\right)=\dfrac{u'v-uv'}{v^2}$, with
        $$\begin{aligned} u&=32\theta & v&=(\theta+2)^2 \\ u'&=32 & v'&=2(\theta+2) \end{aligned}$$
        $$\dfrac{dA}{d\theta}=\dfrac{(\theta+2)^2\cdot 32-32\theta\cdot 2(\theta+2)}{(\theta+2)^4}$$
        For a stationary point $\dfrac{dA}{d\theta}=0$:
        $$\begin{aligned} 32(\theta+2)^2-64\theta(\theta+2)&=0 \\ 32(\theta+2)\left[\theta+2-2\theta\right]&=0 \\ 32(\theta+2)(2-\theta)&=0 \end{aligned}$$
        so $\theta=-2$ or $\theta=2$.

        But $0\le\theta\le\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, and both values lie outside this range, so the maximum occurs at an endpoint.

        At $\theta=0$:
        $$A=0$$
        At $\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$:
        $$\begin{aligned} A&=\dfrac{32\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)}{\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+2\right)^2} \\ &=\dfrac{16\pi}{\left(\frac{\pi+4}{2}\right)^2} \\ &=\dfrac{64\pi}{(\pi+4)^2} \\ &\approx 3.9422\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$
