        \exfigure{995_q15_s1}

        $f(x)=e^{-x}(1+x)$. Using the product rule, $\dfrac{d}{dx}(uv)=u'v+uv'$, with
        $$\begin{aligned} u&=e^{-x} & v&=1+x\\ u'&=-e^{-x} & v'&=1\end{aligned}$$
        $$\begin{aligned}f'(x)&=-e^{-x}(1+x)+e^{-x}\\ &=e^{-x}\left(1-(1+x)\right)\\ &=-xe^{-x}\end{aligned}$$
        For stationary points, $f'(x)=0$: $-xe^{-x}=0$ gives $x=0$, since $e^{-x}\ne0$. Then $f(0)=\dfrac{1+0}{e^0}=1$, giving $(0,1)$.

        To find the nature, differentiate $f'(x)=-xe^{-x}$ with the product rule, with
        $$\begin{aligned} u&=-x & v&=e^{-x}\\ u'&=-1 & v'&=-e^{-x}\end{aligned}$$
        $$\begin{aligned}f''(x)&=-e^{-x}+(-x)\left(-e^{-x}\right)\\ &=e^{-x}(x-1)\end{aligned}$$
        $f''(0)=e^0(-1)=-1<0$, so $(0,1)$ is a local maximum.

        For points of inflexion, $f''(x)=0$: $e^{-x}(x-1)=0$ gives $x=1$, since $e^{-x}\ne0$. Then $f(1)=\dfrac{2}{e}$. Test the concavity either side:

        \extable{Ts2928_1}

        $f''(0)=-1<0$ and $f''(2)=e^{-2}(2-1)=\dfrac{1}{e^2}>0$, so the concavity changes and $\left(1,\dfrac{2}{e}\right)$ is a point of inflexion.

        As $x\to\infty$, $f(x)\to0$, so the $x$-axis is a horizontal asymptote.
