        The graph $y=-f(-x)$ comes from $y=f(x)$ by two reflections: $x\to -x$ is a reflection in the $y$-axis, and the minus sign in front is a reflection in the $x$-axis. Together they rotate the graph $180^\circ$ about the origin.

        The point where $y=f(x)$ touches the $x$-axis, at a positive $x$-value, moves to a point where the new graph touches the $x$-axis at a negative $x$-value.

        The positive $y$-intercept of $y=f(x)$ becomes a negative $y$-intercept.

        As $x\to -\infty$, $y=-f(-x)=-f(\text{large positive})$, which is positive and large, so the left end goes up. As $x\to\infty$, the right end goes down.

        A and B keep the touch point at positive $x$, and C has a positive $y$-intercept, so they fail.
