        \exfigure{571_q21_s1}

        The area of $\triangle PQR$ is the area under $PR$ plus the area under $RQ$, minus the area under $PQ$ (each a trapezium on the $x$-axis).
        $$A=\dfrac{1}{2}\,t\sin t+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}-t\right)\left(\sin t+\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)-\dfrac{1}{2}\times\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\times\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$
        Expand and simplify.
        $$\begin{aligned}A&=\dfrac{1}{2}t\sin t+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\sin t+\dfrac{\sqrt{3}\,\pi}{3}-t\sin t-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t\right)-\dfrac{\pi\sqrt{3}}{6}\\&=\dfrac{\pi}{3}\sin t-\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}t\\&=\dfrac{1}{12}\left(4\pi\sin t-3\sqrt{3}\,t\right)\end{aligned}$$

        Differentiate and find the stationary point.
        $$\begin{aligned}A&=\dfrac{1}{12}\left(4\pi\sin t-3\sqrt{3}\,t\right)\\\dfrac{dA}{dt}&=\dfrac{1}{12}\left(4\pi\cos t-3\sqrt{3}\right)\end{aligned}$$
        Stationary point where $\dfrac{dA}{dt}=0$:
        $$\cos t=\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}$$
        so $t=1.145\ldots$, which lies in $0\le t\le\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.

        Test the nature with the second derivative.
        $$\dfrac{d^2A}{dt^2}=\dfrac{1}{12}\left(-4\pi\sin t\right)$$
        When $t=1.145$, $\dfrac{d^2A}{dt^2}=-0.9534<0$, so the curve is concave down and this is a maximum.
        $$\begin{aligned}A&=\dfrac{1}{12}\left(4\pi\sin 1.145-3\sqrt{3}\times 1.145\right)\\&\approx 0.46\end{aligned}$$
        $$\therefore\ \text{maximum } A\approx 0.46$$
